What it measures

Two axes, measured together: which work inside the discipline pulls a student, and how they want to do that work. The same 250 items carry both, which is why the sitting is twenty minutes and not two.

What goes in

250Interest items Twenty-five inside each of the ten job areas
120Also carry a style Two per style in every area, so a style is asked again in a different situation
3Attention checks Three items that name the answer to give. The report prints whether the sheet passed them

What comes out

10Job areas Scored from 25 items each, so one badly worded item cannot move the result
6Work styles Twenty observations each, gathered across different situations rather than in one block
8Engineering activities Not asked directly. Read off the ten areas through a fixed weight table, which is why the report can show them as a second shape

Every item is a statement about yourself, answered on a five-point scale. Nothing is timed and no answer is the right one: what is read is the pattern across 253 of them, never a single line.

How a sitting runs

Before the itemsChoose a discipline

The job areas belonging to that discipline are the ones used. A mechanical student is never scored against another field's areas.

About 20 minutesThe items, area by area

The style-bearing items sit inside the areas they belong to, two per style per area, rather than waiting in a block at the end. Each answer saves as it is made, so a student can close the tab on a bus and come back that evening.

Three times along the wayAttention checks

Three items name the answer to give: pick Neutral, pick Disagree. They add nothing to any score. What they settle is whether the rest of the sheet can be read at face value, and the report says which way it went.

ImmediatelyScored

Rules, not judgement. The same answer sheet always produces the same scores, and a coordinator can be shown it re-scored.
