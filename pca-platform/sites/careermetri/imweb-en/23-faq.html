What institutions ask first

Does this only work in Korea? No. The instrument, the ten job areas and the six work styles are shared worldwide. What is rebuilt per country is the translation, the alignment to that country's occupational standard and, where wanted, regional employer data. That split is set out under Localisation.
How long does a localisation take? It depends on whether the regional employer module is in scope. Translation and standard alignment are the bulk of it; employer data is the long pole. We scope it against a pilot cohort rather than quoting blind.
Can we run it under our own brand? Yes. A local partner operates the diagnosis in their market under a licence. ACADEMIX supplies the instrument, scoring and report generation; the partner supplies the local data and the relationship with universities.
How do students take it? Two ways, and an institution can use either. A participation link per department, which needs no roster; or a roster upload, which issues each student their own credentials. Large cohorts usually take the second because it lets the coordinator see who has not yet sat it.
Where does student data live? Responses and results are stored server-side. Nothing is kept in the browser. Residency, retention and hand-back at the end of a contract are set out in the agreement and can be arranged to meet local requirements.
What does the institution receive? An anonymised aggregate report covering the cohort's distribution across job areas and work styles, usable as evidence for outcome reporting. Any cell holding fewer than five people shows no number.
Who decides when students see results? The institution. A session can be set to release automatically, or to wait for a coordinator who reads the cohort report first.
How does an institution start? Through the enquiry form. Tell us the country, the discipline and a rough number of students, and we come back with what a pilot would look like. Students never see a payment screen: the agreement creates the seats.
Is it validated? It was built from a 2,346-student sample together with 428 job postings, 137 job descriptions and 62 NCS references, and reviewed by 48 practitioners. Area definitions reference NCS, O*NET, RIASEC and the AERA, APA and NCME measurement standards. The core indicators are registered with the Korea Copyright Commission. Until a norming sample exists we do not report percentiles.
